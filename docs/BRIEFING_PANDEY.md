# Briefing: Prof. Pandey project (Nifty expiry and dealer gamma)

*For Shreyan. Updated 3 November 2026 (about 16:00 IST). Plain-language summary of where the project stands. **Everything below is from real NSE data** (2 January 2023 to 2 November 2026: 942 trading days, 200 Nifty expiry days) unless it says "simulation". Each point names the document with the full detail.*

## The one-paragraph version

We downloaded four years of real NSE data, rebuilt the professor's paper on it, and then tested whether option dealers' hedging (measured as "GEX", dealer gamma exposure) explains expiry-day volatility. The paper's "after" period replicates almost exactly, and we think we found its "before" period to within about a week (64 trading days starting between 14 and 23 November 2022, a year before the dates printed; two independent searches agree on everything except the exact days). A hybrid model beats the paper's model for short-horizon forecasts on Nifty, Bank Nifty and Fin Nifty, and a registered ablation shows that the gain comes from learning from recent volatility and the time of day, not from option-market information. Five plain fixes to the paper's model, written down in advance, do as well as the hybrid on these days, so the gain is over our implementation of the paper's model (which adds a separate intraday curve for expiry days), not over a carefully fixed version of it; because the fixes were chosen after seeing these days, a forward test of them on days from 4 November 2026 is registered. Dealer gamma does not help Nifty forecasts (on Bank Nifty it helped slightly, but only in the 09:15 forecast, and not at all on Fin Nifty), and most in-sample GEX links fall apart under hard testing, with one exception: Nifty reverses into the close, and only after evenings when the "Pro" traders were net short options. That pattern is about Pro traders' positions rather than option gamma, and it is too small to trade. What we cannot pin down with public data is who the dealers are, which is exactly where the professor's knowledge helps.

## Findings, in order of how much to trust them

1. **The data are right.** Minute-bar highs and lows match NSE's official values within 0.05% on 943 of 943 days (highs) and 942 of 943 (lows). Option open interest adds up exactly to NSE's participant totals. ([REAL_DATA_RESULTS.md](REAL_DATA_RESULTS.md), R0)
2. **The paper replicates, with two questions for the authors.** Same 21,239 Nifty returns, same extremes, nearly the same model parameters for the "after" period. The printed "before" dates do not match; a search over every 64-day window found 14 November 2022 to 10 February 2023, which matches the printed maxima to every decimal. A second search, registered in advance and run blind to the first by a separate AI agent, agrees on the year, the length, the return definition and both maxima, but also uses the printed means and prefers 22 November 2022 to 20 February 2023, so we now state the window as starting 14 to 23 November and ending 10 to 21 February. The paper's fall in connectedness (how much each index's volatility is driven by the other's) after the change replicates on that inferred window but not on the printed dates. The paper's hour-by-hour connectedness numbers could not be reproduced by any of 5,440 standard settings in a registered search (P113): the "before" last-hour figure needs each index to be explained more by the other's shocks than by its own, which no normalised or Cholesky setting gives (only settings without normalisation get there, and they then overshoot almost every other number), so we ask the authors how the shares were normalised. ([REPLICATION_NSE.md](REPLICATION_NSE.md), [P108_BLIND_WINDOW.md](P108_BLIND_WINDOW.md), [P113_CONNECTEDNESS_SETTINGS.md](P113_CONNECTEDNESS_SETTINGS.md))
3. **The paper's model can be beaten for the next 15 and 30 minutes** (5.8% and 7.6% better; still 4.8% and 5.9% against a version of the paper's model that also sees India VIX or option prices, but that last edge appears only after November 2024). The winner is a hybrid that keeps the paper's model and corrects its errors with machine learning. Its gain comes from recent volatility (about a third) and the time of day (about a quarter), not from the option market: a registered ablation (P106) finds that removing the option-chain, VIX or calendar inputs costs nothing, and that the edge over the version given option prices appears after November 2024 mostly because that version got worse (on point estimates; that version's change on its own is not significant). ([P106_GAIN_DECOMPOSITION.md](P106_GAIN_DECOMPOSITION.md)) A second registered study (P109) then wrote down five fixes to the paper's model before computing any result. Together they make it 6.8% and 8.3% better at 15 and 30 minutes, which is 118% and 108% of the hybrid's gain (intervals 85% to 167% and 80% to 132%), and the hybrid cannot be told apart from the fixed model. Three fixes do most of the work: giving the minute-by-minute part today's level so far, a correction for each time of day, and letting yesterday's close fade overnight; a HAR daily level helps only inside the combination. The fixed model does not explain the last hour. The fixes were chosen after P106 on the same days, so this explains the hybrid's gain rather than proving the fixed model better; a one-time forward test on Nifty days from 4 November 2026 is registered (P112). ([P109_MCSGARCH_EXTENSIONS.md](P109_MCSGARCH_EXTENSIONS.md), [prereg/P112_FORWARD_MCSGARCH_FIXES.md](prereg/P112_FORWARD_MCSGARCH_FIXES.md)) The hybrid's gain allows for our having tried 31 models, holds when the machine-learning part is retrained with 20 new sets of random seeds, and with nothing changed the hybrid also wins on Bank Nifty, at 3.9% and 5.0%, in a test whose plan was committed before any Bank Nifty forecast was made, and on a third index, Fin Nifty, at 4.9% and 6.1% under its own advance plan (there the last-hour and expiry-day gains were too small to be significant). Because the three indices move together, a pooled test counts them as worth about two independent checks, not three. For the last hour the paper's model given VIX comes within 1.7% of the hybrid's 6.4% gain, so there the hybrid is not shown to beat a fair rival. In money the gain is small: to an investor who sizes a Nifty position by forecast volatility it is worth about 0.04% of wealth a year, and a trading rule built on it loses money after costs. (R1; [MODEL_ITERATIONS_REAL.md](MODEL_ITERATIONS_REAL.md), [REFEREE_RESPONSE_FORECAST.md](REFEREE_RESPONSE_FORECAST.md), [BANKNIFTY_FORECAST.md](BANKNIFTY_FORECAST.md), [THIRD_INDEX_FORECAST.md](THIRD_INDEX_FORECAST.md), [P107_POOLED_THREE_INDEX.md](P107_POOLED_THREE_INDEX.md), [P103_ECONOMIC_VALUE.md](P103_ECONOMIC_VALUE.md))
4. **Expiry days are more volatile at the end of the day, but part of that is noise.** Since Nifty moved its expiry to Tuesday, Tuesday's last hour has 63% more variance than ordinary days on which no index option expires anywhere. Before November 2024 almost every day was some index's expiry day, so no clean comparison exists for that period. When Bank Nifty's expiry moved to Wednesday in 2023, its end-of-day volatility moved with it. But one-minute prices at the end of expiry days bounce back and forth (a sign of bid-ask noise), a noise-robust measure keeps only a half to three quarters of each effect, and the placebo tests have too few independent periods for strong p-values. A synthetic-control study of the November 2024 cut in weekly options was too imprecise to tell. (R2; [EVENT_STUDY.md](EVENT_STUDY.md), [REFEREE_RESPONSE_EXPIRY.md](REFEREE_RESPONSE_EXPIRY.md), [SYNTHETIC_CONTROL.md](SYNTHETIC_CONTROL.md))
5. **Pinning (the index closing near a strike on expiry) happened under the Thursday weekly** (4.5 points closer than on other days, p = 0.0006) but not since the move to Tuesday, and it is unrelated to dealer gamma. None of six market-structure explanations, tested under a plan committed before the results were computed, explains when it comes and goes. ([PINNING_STUDY.md](PINNING_STUDY.md), [PINNING_MECHANISM.md](PINNING_MECHANISM.md))
6. **The one GEX result that held up: the end-of-day reversal.** A 1% move over the day tends to be followed by about a 0.03% move back in the last 30 minutes, and only after evenings when GEX under the Pro rule was negative. It survived shuffles, placebo dates, random sign rules and a split-sample test, but the evenings that drive it come in long stretches (95% of 2023 evenings, about one in five from 2025), so there is less independent evidence than the day count suggests. It is really about Pro traders' positions: asking whether Pro traders were net short index options the evening before gives the same answer, and that agrees with the GEX sign on 916 of 932 evenings. The simplest hedging story (the close continues the day's move) fails outright, and as a trade it earns about zero after costs. ([FALSIFICATION.md](FALSIFICATION.md), [REFEREE_RESPONSE_F2.md](REFEREE_RESPONSE_F2.md), [F2_ECONOMICS.md](F2_ECONOMICS.md))
7. **The GEX results that did not survive.** "Higher GEX, calmer next day" tracks the total amount of option gamma, not who holds it. The "sign flip on expiry day" holds only in sample, only under the Pro rule, and does not appear in Bank Nifty. Expiry-day gamma re-priced minute by minute shows no effect. And we could not identify the dealer sign from the data at all. ([FALSIFICATION.md](FALSIFICATION.md), [INTRADAY_GAMMA.md](INTRADAY_GAMMA.md), [DEALER_SIGN.md](DEALER_SIGN.md))

**How much to trust each finding.** [TEST_LEDGER.md](TEST_LEDGER.md) lists all 28,001 statistical tests the project ran, with a p-value corrected for all of them at once. The forecasting gain over the paper's model survives that correction, as do the Bank Nifty and Fin Nifty replications and the Tuesday last-hour premium; the edge over the information-matched model does not, and the end-of-day reversal survives in sample only. [NUMBER_TRACE.md](NUMBER_TRACE.md) checks that every number in the pitch traces to a results file.

## What went wrong and got fixed (the honest part)

* Three AI referee reports (forecasting, the reversal, the expiry effect) led to weaker but safer claims: the forecasting edge over a fairly informed benchmark is recent, the reversal is a Pro-positioning pattern, and the expiry premium needs a clean baseline and a noise caution. ([REFEREE_RESPONSE_FORECAST.md](REFEREE_RESPONSE_FORECAST.md), [REFEREE_RESPONSE_F2.md](REFEREE_RESPONSE_F2.md), [REFEREE_RESPONSE_EXPIRY.md](REFEREE_RESPONSE_EXPIRY.md))
* An earlier claim that a simpler model was much better for the last hour came from NSE's new closing auction (from 3 August 2026) and was withdrawn.
* Bugs found and fixed with tests: unsorted strikes in NSE's new file format, a holiday-shifted expiry in June 2023, two edge cases in the volatility model, a stale VIX lag and a missing-value coding.

Full log of every iteration: [ITERATION_LOG.md](../ITERATION_LOG.md).

## Decisions taken

* **Every result is reported under three sign rules** (US convention, NSE "Pro" positions, mirror of retail "Client" positions), because nobody can see dealers' positions directly.
* **Out-of-sample forecasting is the headline test,** and every in-sample result went through falsification before being called a finding.
* **Negative results are reported as results.** Most GEX links failed; saying so is what makes the one that survived believable.

## Limitations

* NSE's participant file covers all index options, not just Nifty, and "Pro" includes traders who may not hedge, so the dealer sign is only a proxy.
* End-of-day positions only: we cannot see how positions change during expiry afternoon.
* BSE's servers are not reachable from our cloud setup, so the Sensex expiry dates used as a control are reported, not verified.
* About 100 weekly expiries since November 2024 is enough for 15- and 30-minute forecasts, not for a last-hour-only test, and a simulation calibrated on the real data shows that the GEX effects at half the size we found would need 5 to 11 more years of data (the Tuesday premia at half size need 0.8 to 4.1 more years) ([POWER_REAL.md](POWER_REAL.md)).
