# Real-data results

**Everything in this document comes from REAL market data:** NSE's own files (F&O bhavcopy, participant-wise open interest, daily index values including India VIX), downloaded from NSE's archive server, plus third-party 1-minute Nifty 50 bars from a public GitHub dataset that we checked day by day against NSE's official values. Simulation results are in `docs/EXPERIMENT_REPORT.md` (E1 to E7) and are kept separate.

Sample: 942 trading days, 2 January 2023 to 2 November 2026, of which 200 are Nifty expiry days. Out-of-sample forecasts start after a 150-day training period, so 792 test days. Every number below is copied from a file in `results/`; the file is named next to each table. (The tables were regenerated after a bug fix on 2 November, 20:45 UTC: UDiFF bhavcopies are not sorted by strike, which had affected how missing implied vols were filled. Net GEX changed noticeably on about 100 days where it is close to zero; no conclusion changed, and the largest change in any scoreboard cell was 1.4 points.)

Separate real-data studies, each with its own document:

* `docs/REPLICATION_NSE.md`: the paper's Tables 1 to 6 rebuilt on NSE data. The "after" sample matches closely; the stated "before" window does not, and a 64-day window from 14 November 2022 to 10 February 2023 reproduces the printed "before" numbers (our main question for the authors; a later blind search, P108 below, narrows this to a window starting 14 to 23 November 2022).
* `docs/PINNING_STUDY.md`: a full pinning study (replaces the crude R4 check below).
* `docs/INDEPENDENT_REVIEW.md`: a referee-style review by a separate AI agent (not a human reviewer) of this document and the two above. Its points are addressed here (R1 diagnostics, R3 scale-free versions, multiple-testing columns) or handed to the studies still running.
* `docs/EVENT_STUDY.md`: five expiry rule changes (2019 to 2026) with Nifty and Bank Nifty as each other's comparison and placebo dates. The whole-day expiry premium barely moves with the rules; the end of the day does (Bank Nifty's move to Wednesday raised its last-30-minute premium by 1.14 log points, placebo p 0.005). The per-minute Rogers-Satchell measure the paper uses shows an expiry premium that 5-minute realized variance does not.
* `docs/FALSIFICATION.md`: seven families of tests that try to break R3 and R5 (shuffles, timing placebos, random sign rules, out of sample, Bank Nifty, a quasi-causal roll-off test, multiple testing). R5 survives the shuffles, timing placebos, random sign rules and the split-sample test, and Holm over the 78 headline tests, but not Holm over the study's full family of 742 tests (only its horse-race and 2025-onward versions do); R3's ordinary-day term does not survive.
* `docs/DEALER_SIGN.md`: the dealer sign estimated from the market's response instead of assumed. A documented negative result: the end-of-day outcome points to the reverse of the Pro sign and the volatility outcome points nowhere clearly (the earlier wording "nearly opposite signs" is not established, see `docs/REFEREE_RESPONSE_F2.md` section 6).
* `docs/INTRADAY_GAMMA.md`: expiry-day gamma re-priced minute by minute at the current index level. A clean null on expiry days, also in the final half hour before the close (pre-registered follow-up: 0.26, p = 0.39); the one lead that passed its placebos (gamma concentrated near the index) did not replicate on Bank Nifty (p = 0.66).
* `docs/MODEL_ITERATIONS_REAL.md`: the model study behind R1 (option-implied features, 21-day mcsGARCH refits for every model, 5-seed averaging, a development window and a hold-out the later design choices were not tuned on, a clean GEX ablation with a placebo). R1 below summarises it.
* `docs/CONNECTEDNESS_GAMMA.md`: does Nifty and Bank Nifty volatility spill over more on short-gamma days (a new question for the paper's connectedness design)? The pre-stated Pro-rule test is borderline (+4.29 points on the paper's scale, permutation p = 0.044) and does not survive a VIX control or most robustness checks; total gamma (no sign) goes with lower connectedness (Holm p = 0.010), like the level effect in FALSIFICATION.md.
* `docs/SENSEX_CONFOUND.md`: is BSE's Sensex expiry a hidden confounder? BSE's servers are blocked, so the Sensex calendar is reported, not verified (it matches all 71 option-price expiry dates available). The pre-stated test is not significant (p = 0.16), but Nifty's last 30 minutes are more volatile on Sensex expiry days. With Sensex days left out of the weekly baseline, R2's premia get a little larger (Tuesday last hour +43% becomes +63%), so the published NSE effects were understated, not overstated.
* `docs/F2_ECONOMICS.md`: is R5 worth anything after trading costs? A walk-forward rule whose analysis plan was committed before its results were computed (the test period had already been used to find R5) on 90 test trades from 2025 to July 2026 earns +4.15 bp per trade before costs and -0.89 bp after statutory costs plus 1 bp of slippage per side; it breaks even at about 0.55 bp per side, so after costs it is indistinguishable from zero rather than clearly negative. Verdict by the pre-stated rule: not economically meaningful (a rule whose Deflated Sharpe bar a 90-trade strategy could hardly clear). R5 is a statistical regularity, not a demonstrated trading opportunity.
* `docs/P103_ECONOMIC_VALUE.md`: is the hybrid's forecasting gain worth anything in money? Registered analysis plan (committed before any outcome was computed). For an investor who sizes a Nifty position by forecast volatility every 30 minutes (the yardstick of Bollerslev, Hood, Huss and Pedersen 2018), the hybrid beats mcsGARCH (one-sided p 8.1e-7), but the gain is worth about 0.04% of wealth a year, and against the mcsGARCH given the implied variance it is not significant after Holm (p 0.096). A volatility-targeted intraday rule loses money after costs whatever forecast it uses. Verdict by the pre-stated rule: detectable but not economically meaningful.
* `docs/PINNING_MECHANISM.md`: which market-structure variable explains when pinning appears? Registered analysis plan (committed before this study's outcomes were computed; the pinning pattern itself had been found on the same data). The primary candidate, open interest near the money, has the wrong sign (p = 0.010), so it is ruled out; no candidate explains the regime pattern.
* `docs/TEST_LEDGER.md` (built by `scripts/build_test_ledger.py`): every one of the project's 28,021 hypothesis tests from 33 studies, whether it was pre-specified, its raw p-value and its Benjamini-Yekutieli p-value across the whole project (valid under any dependence between tests); 3,277 survive. Of the findings below, finding 2 survives this correction on all days at every horizon (on expiry days only at 30 minutes, and the edge over an mcsGARCH given the implied variance does not survive at either horizon); findings 6 (the Tuesday last hour), 7 (the in-sample R5 test) and 8 (outcome b) survive in part; finding 5 does not. The primary tests of the Bank Nifty and Fin Nifty replications and of the pooled three-index test (P91 H1, P104 H1, P107 P1) survive; nothing in P92 or P96 does, as their null results imply.
* `docs/POWER_REAL.md`: how large an effect each test could have detected, by simulation on the real data. R5 and R3 had about 80% power only for effects close to the ones estimated; effects half as large would need 5 to 11 more years of data. A GEX sign wrong on a quarter of days cuts power sharply.
* `docs/SENSEX_SWAP.md`: on 1 September 2025 Nifty and Sensex swapped expiry weekdays (Nifty to Tuesday, Sensex to Thursday). Registered analysis plan (committed before any outcome of this study was computed, on data not used before for this question): does the expiring index become the net transmitter of minute-level volatility spillovers on its own expiry day? Over the whole day, no (+0.42 points, permutation p = 0.33). In the last hour the shift is in that direction (+2.51 points, Holm p = 0.015), but a fake swap date in 2025 also moved the measure (+4.45, p = 0.038), so the design check failed and the last-hour sign needs new data.
* `docs/SYNTHETIC_CONTROL.md` (P96): when SEBI ended weekly options on Bank Nifty, Fin Nifty and Midcap Select in November 2024, did their old expiry weekday calm down? Registered analysis plan (committed before any outcome was computed). Each index is compared with a synthetic version built from 19 NSE sector indices that have no options, using a daily high-low volatility measure. Bank Nifty's Wednesday: no support (+0.12 log points, the wrong sign, placebo p = 0.95), but the study could only have seen effects of about 1.4 log points, so the honest reading is "too imprecise to tell". A post hoc observation matters more: before the change the option-free sector indices were also more volatile on Wednesdays than in the rest of their week (+0.28, against Bank Nifty's +0.19), so Bank Nifty's whole-day Wednesday premium cannot be told apart from a market-wide Wednesday effect. This fits R2: the expiry effect that holds up is at the end of the day, not over the whole day.
* `docs/BANKNIFTY_FORECAST.md` (P91): does the Nifty forecasting hybrid beat mcsGARCH on Bank Nifty with nothing changed? Registered analysis plan (committed before any Bank Nifty forecast or loss was computed). **Replicated**: 3.9% and 5.0% lower QLIKE at 15 and 30 minutes (Holm p 5e-12 and 2e-11), about 70% of Nifty's gain over the same dates; last hour 8.1% (p 3e-7). The pre-stated prediction that GEX would not help failed: at 15 minutes GEX features improve the hybrid by 0.6% to 0.8% under every sign rule while a placebo does not, and all of it is at the 09:15 forecast (an exploratory finding that needs its own test). Caveats: not independent of Nifty (same market, same days), India VIX stands in for a Bank Nifty VIX, and after November 2024 Bank Nifty has only monthly options.
* `docs/SEED_BOOTSTRAP.md` (P99): does the forecasting headline depend on LightGBM's random seeds (the referee's estimation-noise item, M4.3)? Registered analysis plan (committed before any outcome was computed). The hybrid was refitted with 20 new, non-overlapping sets of 5 seeds, everything else unchanged; the headline's own seed reproduces the committed forecasts exactly. **The headline holds**: all 20 refits beat mcsGARCH at 15 and 30 minutes on all days, by 5.64% to 5.92% (largest p 1.1e-8) and 7.76% to 8.11% (largest p 1.8e-13); a bootstrap over days and seeds gives p below 0.001 at both. At 30 minutes the headline's 7.65% is the smallest gain of all 21 seed sets. The last hour holds on all days (largest p 0.00043) but is weakened on expiry days: 19 of 20 refits have p below 0.05, the worst 0.052, median 0.026, so the headline's 0.010 there was the most favourable of all 21 seed sets. Seeds move the margin by 0.075 to 0.244 points (standard deviation, all days), about a tenth of the day-sampling noise. Caveat: seeds are only one source of estimation noise; refit dates and hyperparameters were held fixed.
* `docs/THIRD_INDEX_FORECAST.md` (P104): does the hybrid also beat mcsGARCH on a third index? Registered analysis plan (committed before any Fin Nifty forecast or loss was computed). Fin Nifty was the only third NSE index with free 1-minute bars that pass the same check against NSE's official daily values. **Replicated** at the primary horizons: run with nothing changed, the hybrid is 4.9% and 6.1% better at 15 and 30 minutes on 604 test days (Holm p 4.4e-12 for both), close to Nifty's 5.1% and 6.6% on the same dates. The secondary predictions are mixed: the last-hour gain is not supported (1.0%, p 0.30; it appears only after November 2024), the expiry-day gain points the right way but is not significant (3.9% and 5.3% on 82 days, Holm p 0.093), and GEX features did not help under any sign rule (smallest Holm p 0.40), so the Bank Nifty 09:15 GEX gain did not reappear. Post hoc: on 5 June 2024, the day after the general-election result, mcsGARCH's 09:15 forecast was 135 times the realized variance on Fin Nifty (20 times on Nifty); that one forecast is 98% of mcsGARCH's 15-minute squared error on Fin Nifty (79% on Nifty, 42% on Bank Nifty), so squared-error (MSE) comparisons against mcsGARCH mostly measure that one morning, while the QLIKE results hardly change without it. Caveats: Fin Nifty moves with Bank Nifty and Nifty, it borrows Nifty's VIX, and its minute bars come from a GitHub repository that states no licence or origin.
* `docs/P107_POOLED_THREE_INDEX.md` (P107): one test for all three indices at once. Registered analysis plan (committed before any pooled statistic was computed; each index's own result was already known, so it fixes only the pooling method and is a summary, not new evidence). On the 604 dates common to Nifty, Bank Nifty and Fin Nifty, with Driscoll-Kraay standard errors that allow for the indices moving together and for dependence over days, the hybrid's loss is 4.7% and 6.0% lower than mcsGARCH's at 15 and 30 minutes (t 10.7 and 11.3; a joint bootstrap over dates agrees), and the three gains cannot be told apart (p 0.23 and 0.37). The hybrid's daily advantage is correlated across indices (0.22 Nifty with Bank Nifty, 0.41 Nifty with Fin Nifty, 0.60 Bank Nifty with Fin Nifty, at 15 minutes), so the three indices are worth about 1.8 independent ones at 15 minutes and 2.4 at 30 minutes; added last, Fin Nifty contributes almost nothing. Pooled, the edge over the mcsGARCH given the implied variance is 2.5% and 3.0%, only just significant within the study (Holm p 0.043) and not after the project-wide correction. In the last hour the indices differ clearly (Fin Nifty about 1%).
* `docs/P108_BLIND_WINDOW.md` (P108): a blind re-derivation of the paper's "before" window. Registered plan committed before any search on real data (6b289ca); a separate AI agent typed the printed numbers itself, wrote new code, and pushed its answer (d904c8c, 21 minutes later) before it could see the earlier search. It scored 132,126 windows under 204 return definitions on how many of 15 printed numbers they reproduce exactly. The stated window (November 2023 to February 2024) reproduces none under any definition and ranks 85,013th. The best window is 22 November 2022 to 20 February 2023 (6 of 15 exact, including both printed means; the window one day later ties), with the same return definition and the same two maxima as the earlier search. The earlier answer, 14 November 2022 to 10 February 2023, ranks 9th: it misses the Bank Nifty mean (-0.00000055, which prints as -0.000001, not -0.000002), while the precise higher moments favour starts of 14 to 17 November. Joint reading: 64 trading days starting 14 to 23 November 2022 and ending 10 to 21 February 2023; the two candidates share 58 days. Only the authors can settle the exact days.
* `docs/P113_CONNECTEDNESS_SETTINGS.md` (P113): which connectedness setting reproduces the paper's 15 printed totals (Tables 3 and 4)? Registered plan committed before any connectedness number was computed (313a3ac; results c764984), run by an AI agent. Grid of 5,440 settings per before window: generalized decompositions normalised by row, by column or not at all, Cholesky in both orders, 8 input series, 8 horizons and 17 ways of building the sample and choosing the VAR lag. Verdict "not reproduced". The closest (column-normalised generalized decomposition of the log mcsGARCH variance, days joined end to end, VAR(5), horizon 5) misses the 15 totals by 5.34 points on average, 8 of 15 within 2 points; it fits better than the same totals shuffled among the cells (permutation p 0.003) but misses the before last hour by 23.1 points (printed 105.22). No normalised (by row or by column) or Cholesky setting gives that cell more than 94.66, and none has both cross shares above 43.80%, against printed 52.79% and 52.43%; unnormalised shares pass 100 in almost every cell. The 10 after-sample totals are fitted no better than chance (p 0.63). Under the closest setting the fall on expiry days holds (-7.95 and -10.00 points, Holm p 0.012) but the larger fall in the last hour does not (intervals include zero). Question for the authors: were the shares normalised so that each row sums to 100, and with which input, VAR lag and horizon?
* `docs/P114_PERSISTENCE_FIXED_MODEL.md` (P114): does the paper's fall in intraday persistence survive when mcsGARCH includes P109's fixes X1 to X3 (a moving intraday level, an overnight fade and a HAR daily level)? Registered plan committed before any extended model was estimated on these windows (86e3866; results 7c1fc13), run by an AI agent. Primary measure: the half-life of the impulse response to one shock at 12:15. Verdict "survives" for Nifty on the inferred window: the half-life falls from 141 to 18 minutes (reference model 359 to 72), change in equivalent persistence -0.033 (day-block bootstrap 95% interval -0.058 to -0.003, one-sided p 0.022), but the share of the original fall that is kept is very uncertain (1.29, interval -0.04 to 6.91). Not confirmed for Bank Nifty (bootstrap p 0.105), weakened on P108's window, and on the stated window the fixed model shows no fall for Nifty (19.6 to 17.9 minutes) and a rise for Bank Nifty. Across 44 rolling Nifty windows the fixed model's half-life is largest in the inferred before window (median 25), so the fall may be a return from an unusually persistent period at the end of 2022. The primary test does not survive the project-wide correction.
* `docs/P106_GAIN_DECOMPOSITION.md` (P106): where the forecasting hybrid's gain comes from. Registered plan committed before any ablated model's loss was computed (71bb3b6; results 149b358). The hybrid was refitted five times, each without one input group, with five seed sets on Nifty. Realized-volatility history carries 34% and 38% of the gain at 15 and 30 minutes and the time of day 23% and 25%; the option-implied, VIX and calendar groups add nothing, and a hybrid given only mcsGARCH's own inputs is 6.1% and 8.0% better than mcsGARCH. Across the November 2024 cut the hybrid's own gain was steady (p 0.45 and 0.29), while the mcsGARCH given the implied variance went from 4.98 and 6.06 points better than mcsGARCH to 1.06 and 0.64 points worse, which is 87% and 82% of the change in the hybrid's edge over it. No group's contribution changed significantly under the registered rule (closest: the implied group at 30 minutes, Holm 0.051, which a seed-only placebo shows this test can overstate). LightGBM's split-gain importance, which ranks the implied inputs highly, should not be read as what the hybrid would lose without them.
* `docs/P109_MCSGARCH_EXTENSIONS.md` (P109): can plain fixes to mcsGARCH recover the hybrid's gain? Registered plan committed before any extended model was run on real data (ceec2ec; results 7f0b5c7). Five fixes, each aimed at one suspected mistake, plus their combination: X1 a HAR daily level, X2 an overnight fade of the minute-by-minute part, X3 today's running level fed to the minute-by-minute part, X4 leverage, X5 one calibration factor per forecast time. On 792 Nifty test days the combination is 6.83 and 8.25 points better than mcsGARCH at 15 and 30 minutes (one-sided Diebold-Mariano t 5.61 and 5.01), 118% and 108% of the hybrid's gain (95% stationary-bootstrap intervals 85% to 167% and 80% to 132%; registered verdict "supported: recovers most of the gain"), and the hybrid does not beat it (p 0.823 and 0.729). Alone, X3 recovers 46% and 48%, X5 49% and 39%, X2 12% and 16%, X1 19% and 3%, and X4 nothing. At the 09:15 forecast the combination gains 28.7 and 34.4 points against the hybrid's 13.3 and 23.9; at the other forecast times it recovers 93%. It does not explain the last hour (13%). With nothing retuned it recovers 127% and 136% on Bank Nifty and 97% and 103% on Fin Nifty. The fixes were chosen after P106 on the same days, so this explains the hybrid's gain; it does not show the fixed model better on new data. That test is registered as P112 (`docs/prereg/P112_FORWARD_MCSGARCH_FIXES.md`, commit f081138): one-sided Diebold-Mariano at 15 and 30 minutes, Holm at 5%, run once at the 250th valid Nifty day from 4 November 2026 or 31 December 2027.
* `docs/REFEREE_RESPONSE_F2.md`: post hoc answers to an AI referee on finding 7. The proxy-free hedging test (dealers short all options, so the close continues the day's move) fails: the end-of-day slope is -0.029 (a reversal). With inference built for the slow negative-Pro state, F2 holds over the full sample (largest robust p 0.012) and from 2025 (0.038) but not inside 2023 to 2024; only the Pro rule shows it.
* `docs/REKEY_CHECK.md`: a separate AI agent re-typed all 223 printed numbers from the paper blind; 222 matched, and the one difference is a typesetting slip in the paper.

**What "pre-registered" means in this project.** Two kinds of advance commitment are used, and they are not equally strong. (1) **Registered analysis plans** (P87 pinning mechanism, P90 F2 economics, P92 Nifty and Sensex swap, and P96 synthetic control, P91 Bank Nifty forecasting, P99 seed bootstrap, P103 economic value, P104 Fin Nifty forecasting, P107 pooled three-index test, P106 where the forecasting gain comes from, P108 blind re-derivation of the before window, P109 five fixes to mcsGARCH, P113 connectedness settings): the plan was committed to git before that study's outcomes were computed, and the result commit came minutes to hours later. This rules out choosing the analysis after seeing its result. It does not make the data new: P87 and P90 test patterns that had been found on the same days. (2) **The forward tests** ([prereg/FORWARD_TEST.md](prereg/FORWARD_TEST.md), commit 5994e63, and P112, [prereg/P112_FORWARD_MCSGARCH_FIXES.md](prereg/P112_FORWARD_MCSGARCH_FIXES.md), commit f081138): committed before any of their data existed, so they are the only genuinely prospective tests. Commit times come from our own machine's clock and GitHub's push record; nothing was lodged with a third-party registry. Who did the work: the code, analyses and documents were produced with AI assistance (Claude, Anthropic) under Shreyan's direction, and most commits list Claude as author; the "independent review", the blind re-key and the referee report were done by separate AI agents, not by people.

## Findings (short)

1. **The data hold up.** Minute-bar highs and lows match NSE's official values within 0.05% on 943 of 943 days (highs) and 942 of 943 (lows) for Nifty. Bhavcopy open interest is in shares and adds up exactly to NSE's participant totals once contracts expiring that day are left out.
2. **Our implementation of the paper's model can be beaten out of sample at 15 and 30 minutes, and more weakly when it is also given India VIX or option prices; most of the gain comes at the open.** A hybrid that keeps mcsGARCH (our implementation of the paper's model, with an added expiry-day intraday curve) and corrects it with a LightGBM model of its errors forecasts the next 15 minutes, the next 30 minutes and the last hour 5.8%, 7.6% and 6.4% better on all days and 6.6%, 6.2% and 7.9% better on expiry days (QLIKE loss; one-sided Diebold-Mariano p at most 0.010 in all six cells), against an mcsGARCH refitted as often as every other model. It holds before the closing auction (5.5%, 6.9%, 7.0%) and on a later hold-out (6.4%, 9.2%, 7.1%; it checks the later tuning, not the choice of the hybrid). Checks made after an AI referee report (post hoc, [REFEREE_RESPONSE_FORECAST.md](REFEREE_RESPONSE_FORECAST.md)): the 15- and 30-minute gains survive Hansen's SPA test for having tried 31 challengers, and they hold against an mcsGARCH also given India VIX or the option-implied variance as its daily level (post hoc, concentrated since November 2024, and not robust to the project-wide correction in [TEST_LEDGER.md](TEST_LEDGER.md)): 4.8% (p 0.005) and 5.9% (p below 0.001). The hybrid structure itself is not shown to matter: plain LightGBM cannot be told apart from it over all days (p 0.32 to 0.97). **Where the gain comes from** (P106, registered analysis plan, [P106_GAIN_DECOMPOSITION.md](P106_GAIN_DECOMPOSITION.md)): refitting the hybrid without each of five input groups, the recent realized-volatility history carries 34% and 38% of the gain at 15 and 30 minutes and the time of day 23% and 25% (Holm p below 0.01, all five seed sets agree); removing the option-implied, VIX or calendar groups costs nothing (removing the calendar inputs even helps slightly), and a hybrid given only mcsGARCH's own inputs is 6.1% and 8.0% better than mcsGARCH. The edge over the information-matched mcsGARCH appears after November 2024 mostly, on point estimates, because that benchmark lost its advantage (82% to 87% of the change, although that benchmark's own change is not significant); the hybrid's own gain did not change significantly. The same two groups lead on Bank Nifty and Fin Nifty. **A fixed mcsGARCH matches it** (P109, registered analysis plan, [P109_MCSGARCH_EXTENSIONS.md](P109_MCSGARCH_EXTENSIONS.md)): five fixes to mcsGARCH written down in advance (chiefly today's running level, a time-of-day calibration and an overnight fade) together recover 118% and 108% of the hybrid's gain (intervals 85% to 167% and 80% to 132%), and the hybrid cannot be told apart from them (p 0.823 and 0.729), so the hybrid's gain is over the paper's mcsGARCH, not over a carefully fixed one. The fixes were chosen after P106 on the same days; their forward test is registered (P112). **It replicates on Bank Nifty** (P91, registered analysis plan, [BANKNIFTY_FORECAST.md](BANKNIFTY_FORECAST.md)): run with nothing changed on 660 Bank Nifty test days, the hybrid is 3.9% and 5.0% better than mcsGARCH at 15 and 30 minutes (Holm p 5e-12 and 2e-11), 8.1% in the last hour, and 2.8% and 3.4% on Bank Nifty expiry days. The gain is largest at 09:15 (13% and 24%), where mcsGARCH leans on yesterday's daily variance, and is still 5% to 6% at all later times of day and in each of the three expiry regimes at 15 and 30 minutes. mcsGARCH is excluded from the Model Confidence Set at 15 and 30 minutes. Under squared-error loss (MSE) the ranking reverses, because mcsGARCH and the hybrid made a few very large errors right after the 4 June 2024 election-result crash, but none of those MSE differences is significant (R1, [MODEL_ITERATIONS_REAL.md](MODEL_ITERATIONS_REAL.md)).
3. **The last hour: a withdrawn claim and a smaller, sturdier one.** The first run seemed to show HAR 11% better than mcsGARCH for the last hour; most of that came from the 63 days after NSE introduced a closing auction on 3 August 2026 (see R0), and it was withdrawn. In the model study (all protocol changes together; which one made the difference has not been isolated) the hybrid is 6.4% better than mcsGARCH (p below 0.001), 7.0% before the auction (p = 0.002), and HAR is not better at all (+1.9%). Treat it as the least secure of the three horizons: the hybrid wins on only 54% of days, it gains nothing in the Thursday-expiry regime, and the Model Confidence Set for the last hour keeps 27 of 32 models. Post hoc, an mcsGARCH with India VIX as its daily level comes within 1.7% of the hybrid (p 0.23), so in the last hour the hybrid is not shown to beat a fairer benchmark (the hybrid itself gets this gain mainly from recent realized volatility, 69%, not from VIX, 6%; P106), and on expiry days no model beats mcsGARCH in the last hour once the 31 challengers are allowed for (step-down p 0.062; [REFEREE_RESPONSE_FORECAST.md](REFEREE_RESPONSE_FORECAST.md)).
4. **GEX features do not improve out-of-sample forecasts** under any of the three sign rules, and signed GEX is never better than unsigned gross gamma. Adding GEX to the hybrid moves its loss by -0.4% to +0.1% at 15 and 30 minutes and makes the last hour 0.9% to 1.6% worse; no cell on all days, expiry days or before the auction survives Holm. This matches what our simulations (E4) said to expect when the sign of dealer positions is uncertain and the effect is small.
5. **In-sample, Pro-based GEX is related to volatility with a sign that flips on expiry day, but the falsification tests weaken this a lot.** On ordinary days, higher dealer gamma the evening before goes with lower volatility the next day, as hedging theory predicts; on expiry days the relation reverses. Under the US and Client-mirror rules it does not survive scale-free versions of GEX, so the earlier statement that it holds under all three rules was too strong. We then tried hard to break it ([FALSIFICATION.md](FALSIFICATION.md)). The ordinary-day part failed: a 20-day block shuffle of GEX gives p = 0.25, GEX measured on later days predicts as well as yesterday's, 91.9% of randomly drawn sign rules are also significant, and gross gamma alone (no sign at all) gives t = -5.3. So it behaves like a slow-moving level of total gamma, not like a dealer signal. The expiry-day flip under the Pro rule survived the shuffles, the random sign rules and a timing horse race (Holm p = 0.020 among the 18 headline tests), but adding it makes out-of-sample forecasts worse (squared error +5%), it does not replicate on Bank Nifty (p = 0.25), and it is carried by the daily Pro positioning weights from the participant file rather than by the option chain. It is an in-sample association on a proxied sign, not a causal or forecasting result (R3).
6. **Expiry days are more volatile than the rest of their week, but not in every regime.** About 11% more realized variance before November 2024 (p = 0.045, but the test over-rejects in that period, so this is weak), no clear difference from November 2024 to August 2025 (Thursday expiry, one weekly per exchange), and about 21% more since Nifty moved to Tuesday, concentrated in the last hour (about 43% more; a stricter subsample in the event study puts the whole-day Tuesday premium near zero but confirms the end-of-day premium, see R2). BSE's Sensex expiry days were raising the weekly baseline; leaving them out makes every premium a little larger (Tuesday last hour about 63%; [SENSEX_CONFOUND.md](SENSEX_CONFOUND.md), calendar reported, not verified). Post hoc, against a baseline of days on which no NSE or BSE index option expires ([REFEREE_RESPONSE_EXPIRY.md](REFEREE_RESPONSE_EXPIRY.md)): the Tuesday premium is +26% for the whole day (p 0.0009) and +63% for the last hour; the pre-November 2024 premium cannot be identified (only 17 usable weeks, -11%, p 0.17), because from September 2023 to November 2024 a week had on average 0.10 expiry-free days. On expiry days the last-30-minute one-minute returns are negatively autocorrelated (bid-ask noise), and a realized kernel keeps a half to three quarters of each end-of-day premium.
7. **Nifty reverses into the close rather than continuing, and the reversal is stronger on days when the Pro-based GEX is negative.** This is the opposite of what the US literature on hedging-driven momentum predicts. It is the most robust GEX result in the project, with limits: it survives block shuffles (p = 0.0005 to 0.010), GEX from the next day does not predict it, Holm p = 0.016 among the 78 headline tests, and a split-sample test on 2025 to 2026 data with the direction taken from 2023 to 2024 (p = 0.006, split chosen after the full-sample result), and it is the same without the closing-auction days (R5). It does not survive Holm over the falsification study's full family of 742 tests, the negative-Pro evenings come in a few long runs (so the effective sample is small, and with inference built for that it holds over the full sample and from 2025 but not inside 2023 to 2024, [REFEREE_RESPONSE_F2.md](REFEREE_RESPONSE_F2.md)), and it is carried by the Pro participant weights rather than the option chain, so it is a Pro-positioning pattern more than a dealer-gamma one (post hoc: the state "Pro net short index options" agrees with the GEX sign on 916 of 932 evenings and gives the same result, and the gamma weighting adds nothing measurable; [REFEREE_RESPONSE_F2.md](REFEREE_RESPONSE_F2.md), section 8). After costs a rule with a registered plan earns about zero ([F2_ECONOMICS.md](F2_ECONOMICS.md)).
8. **The data do not reveal one dealer sign that fits both predictions of hedging theory.** When the dealer sign is estimated from the market's response instead of assumed ([DEALER_SIGN.md](DEALER_SIGN.md)), the volatility outcome points weakly to the Pro sign (p = 0.18) and the end-of-day outcome points strongly to the reverse of the Pro sign (p = 4e-6). Out of sample, no estimated sign beats the three fixed rules after correcting for multiple tests. In other words, finding 7 fits hedging theory only if the Pro rule has the wrong sign, and finding 5 fits only if it has the right one; the honest summary is that the participant-based sign is a proxy whose meaning we cannot pin down with public data.

## R1. Out-of-sample forecasting contest

**This section reports the second, stronger contest** (the "model study", full detail in [MODEL_ITERATIONS_REAL.md](MODEL_ITERATIONS_REAL.md), files in `results/pipeline_nse_v2/`). It replaced the first real-data run on 2 November 2026 (23:00 UTC). What changed, and why: mcsGARCH is now refitted every 21 trading days like every other model (the first run refitted it only every 63 days, which handicapped the paper's model; the 21-day version is 1.6% to 2.1% better, so the benchmark got stronger); the base features gained option-implied information (the ATM straddle and its term structure); the last-hour models are trained on every 60-minute window of the day; every LightGBM forecast is the average of 5 random seeds; Diebold-Mariano p-values use a Student t; two small look-ahead problems were removed (the order-flow scale was a whole-sample median, and the placebo could draw later days); and two data fixes were applied (a holiday-shifted expiry in June 2023, and the monthly-expiry flag now taken from futures expiries). The design was chosen on test days up to 30 June 2025 only; the days after that (the "hold-out") were evaluated once. One qualification: the first run, whose results were known when the model study began, covered the hold-out days too, so the hold-out is data the new design choices were not tuned on, not data nobody had looked at. The first run is kept unchanged in `results/pipeline_nse/` as an archive, because the live forecast of 3 November was frozen from it.

Setup: forecasts of realized variance (sum of squared 1-minute returns) over the next 15 minutes and next 30 minutes from every 15-minute mark, and over the last hour from 14:30. Every model is refitted every 21 trading days on all data before the forecast block (expanding window, first forecast after 150 training days), so no model ever sees the future. Loss: QLIKE (lower is better; it punishes forecasts that are too low more than forecasts that are too high, and is the standard loss for variance forecasts), shown as % difference from mcsGARCH, with the one-sided Diebold-Mariano p-value that the model has lower loss than mcsGARCH in brackets. **Is the improvement real?**

* **Allowing for the search over 31 challengers** (post hoc, [REFEREE_RESPONSE_FORECAST.md](REFEREE_RESPONSE_FORECAST.md)): Hansen's SPA test rejects "no challenger beats mcsGARCH" in all 9 horizon and subset cells (p at most 0.040), and the hybrid's step-down adjusted p-value is below 0.05 in 8 of the 9 (not the last hour on expiry days, 0.062). White's Reality Check, which is not studentized, rejects in none.
* **Against an mcsGARCH also given VIX or implied variance** (post hoc): the best of four versions that use India VIX or the option-implied variance (chosen after seeing the results, which favours the benchmark) is still beaten by 4.8% (p 0.005) at 15 minutes and 5.9% (p below 0.001) at 30 minutes (Giacomini-White tests show this edge comes from the period since November 2024, about zero before, and does not concentrate on expiry days), and by 3.7% and 3.9% for a ridge regression on the hybrid's features, but in the last hour mcsGARCH with VIX as its daily level is within 1.7% (p 0.23).
* **Before the closing auction** (729 test days; see R0): 5.5%, 6.9% and 7.0% better at 15 minutes, 30 minutes and the last hour (expiry days 5.4%, 5.4% and 6.5%; all p at most 0.036). The 63 auction days are reported separately in MODEL_ITERATIONS_REAL.md, Section 4.2, and not interpreted (13 expiry days).
* **Hold-out** (1 July 2025 onwards, 330 test days, under the final configuration): 6.4%, 9.2% and 7.1% better, against 5.3%, 6.6% and 5.3% on the development window where the later design choices were made. Two limits: the hybrid itself became the headline model after winning the first run, which covered these days too, so the hold-out checks the later tuning choices, not the choice of model; and in the hold-out plain LightGBM beats the hybrid at every horizon (7.7%, 10.4% and 12.9% better than mcsGARCH), although over all days the two cannot be told apart (p 0.32 to 0.97).
* **Day by day:** the hybrid's daily loss is below mcsGARCH's on 71% of test days at 15 minutes, 74% at 30 minutes and 54% for the last hour. At 15 and 30 minutes this is a steady edge on most days; in the last hour it is a smaller edge built on fewer, larger wins.
* **Model Confidence Set** (10% level): at 15 and 30 minutes it keeps 18 of 32 models on all days, every one LightGBM-based plus one ridge model, and excludes mcsGARCH and HAR. For the last hour it keeps 27 of 32: with one forecast per day the last hour separates models poorly, so the hybrid's last-hour edge over mcsGARCH is real by the pairwise test but the data cannot rank the best few models there.

**Where the gain comes from** (MODEL_ITERATIONS_REAL.md, Section 4.4; `gain_by_anchor_*.csv`). The 09:15 forecast has to absorb the overnight news, and mcsGARCH handles it with a fixed time-of-day factor times yesterday's daily level. The hybrid is 13.3% (15 minutes) and 23.9% (30 minutes) better at 09:15, and still 5.1% and 6.0% better at all later anchors (p below 0.001); the open is 4% of forecasts and brings 20% and 29% of the hybrid's total gain. With each model's worst 1% of losses removed it is 5.6%, 6.7% and 6.4% better. By expiry regime (several weeklies to November 2024, one Thursday weekly, Tuesday weekly) it is better in all three at 15 and 30 minutes (5.2% to 9.7%, every p at most 0.002); in the last hour it gains nothing in the Thursday regime (+0.0%) and gains in the other two (9.9% and 7.1%). Plain LightGBM is different: no better than mcsGARCH before November 2024 and much better afterwards, and mostly an opening-forecast model.

**MSE tells a different story, for a specific reason.** Under mean squared error, HAR, VIX and plain LightGBM all have about 85% lower MSE than mcsGARCH at 15 and 30 minutes, and the hybrid 27% to 33% lower. Ten anchors account for 97% of mcsGARCH's squared error at 15 minutes, led by 5 June 2024 at 09:15, the morning after the election-result crash: mcsGARCH forecast a 15-minute variance of 0.00201 against a realized 0.000099, about 20 times too high; the hybrid, which multiplies mcsGARCH by a correction, forecast 0.00163; plain LightGBM 0.000008. The version of mcsGARCH whose daily level comes from the straddle forecast 0.000228, which points at the EGARCH daily component as the source. None of these MSE differences is significant (p 0.13 to 0.18) and the MSE Model Confidence Set keeps 30 to 32 of the 32 models, so MSE gives no reliable ranking here. QLIKE is the better loss for comparing variance forecasts (Patton 2011), but a risk manager hurt mostly by the rare crash morning should not read the QLIKE ranking as the answer to their question.

**Does GEX help? No.** The clean test compares the same model with and without a block of GEX features, under each sign rule, plus unsigned gross gamma and a placebo (GEX from a random earlier day). Adding GEX to the hybrid changes its loss by -0.42% to +0.14% at 15 and 30 minutes and by +0.9% to +1.6% (worse) in the last hour on all days. On all days, on expiry days and before the auction, no cell survives the Holm adjustment, and signed GEX is never significantly better than unsigned gross gamma. The only cells that pass Holm are at 30 minutes in the hold-out (about -0.6% to -1.1%), but there the placebo also "helps" and the development window shows nothing, so we read them as noise (MODEL_ITERATIONS_REAL.md, Section 4.5). The sign rule does not matter.

**What the mcsGARCH fit looks like on the whole sample** (`mcsgarch_full_sample.csv`): intraday GARCH $\alpha = 0.049$, $\beta = 0.945$, so persistence 0.994 and an implied half-life of about 2 hours; Student-$t$ degrees of freedom 5.9. Across the walk-forward refits $\alpha$ ranges from 0.025 to 0.052 (`mcsgarch_params.csv`).

**What the first run said, and what changed.** The first run (63-day mcsGARCH refits, no implied features, single-seed LightGBM; `results/pipeline_nse/`) found the hybrid 6.7% and 9.0% better at 15 and 30 minutes and nothing reliably better than mcsGARCH in the last hour, where HAR's apparent 11% edge came mostly from the closing-auction days and was withdrawn. The 15- and 30-minute conclusion is unchanged (the margins are a point or so smaller against the stronger benchmark). The last-hour conclusion changed: under the final protocol the hybrid has a 6.4% edge (p below 0.001) that also holds before the auction (7.0%, p = 0.002), and HAR is now 1.9% worse than mcsGARCH. We have not isolated which change caused this. Training on every 60-minute window alone did not move the hybrid on the development window (MODEL_ITERATIONS_REAL.md, Iteration 6); the gap appeared once all the final changes were applied together (Iteration 10), and it is a little larger in the hold-out (7.1%) than on the development window (5.3%, p = 0.048).

**Caveats.** Minute volume does not exist for the index before 2026, so the volume feature is constant and the "order-flow" feature is a signed-return proxy, not true order flow. The GEX features are built from end-of-day chains only. The regime label is a LightGBM feature, so in the first block after each rule change the model has not seen that label. Implied variance is per trading day and the risk-free rate is fixed at 6.5%. In 2 of the 38 refits (the ones trained on the first 171 and 192 days, which forecast 42 test days in August to October 2023) the daily EGARCH inside mcsGARCH failed to converge on so short a sample and the code fell back to an EWMA daily variance (lambda 0.94); this is recorded in `mcsgarch_params.csv` (column `daily_method`) and affects mcsGARCH and every model built on it in those weeks. Post hoc we found that the rule which triggers this fallback also looked at days after the test block, so it was not a real-time rule (in the refit trained on 192 days it fired only because of 21 April 2025). A strictly real-time rule makes the benchmark weaker (5.5% and 6.9% worse at 15 and 30 minutes), and leaving out the 42 affected days the hybrid's margins are 5.9%, 7.9% and 6.5% ([REFEREE_RESPONSE_FORECAST.md](REFEREE_RESPONSE_FORECAST.md)), so the conclusion does not depend on it. The DM test treats forecasts as fixed: a single LightGBM seed moves a model by up to 0.4 points, and a DM test between two seeds of the identical model gave p = 0.024 (Section 4.7 of the model study), so sub-1-point differences are not reliable even when p is small. Holm is applied within each ablation family, not across the whole study; the project-wide correction is in [TEST_LEDGER.md](TEST_LEDGER.md).

## R2. Expiry-day volatility by expiry regime

So the robust Tuesday result is the end-of-day premium; the whole-day premium depends on which weeks and which measure are used.

## R3. Is yesterday's GEX related to today's volatility? (in-sample)

Regression of the log of today's realized variance on yesterday's log realized variance, its 5-day average, the log of yesterday's VIX, an expiry-day dummy, yesterday's net GEX (divided by its 20-day median gross GEX; this ratio runs from -0.31 to +0.62 under the US rule but only from -0.10 to +0.10 under the Pro rule and from -0.09 to +0.19 under the Client mirror, so coefficients are not comparable across rules) and GEX times the expiry dummy. Newey-West standard errors with 5 lags, 937 days. File: `results/real_day_level/r3_gex_regressions.csv`. Shown with controls for regime (each regime has its own level and its own expiry effect), because the participant-based GEX drifts upward over the sample (figure `results/pipeline_nse/figures/gex_by_sign_rule.png`).

| Sign rule | GEX on ordinary days (coefficient, p) | Extra GEX effect on expiry days (coefficient, p) | Total effect on expiry days |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| US convention | -0.38 (p = 0.030) | +0.39 (p = 0.11) | about 0 |
| Pro participant | -2.07 (p = 0.033) | +4.93 (p = 0.002) | +2.86 |
| Mirror of Client | -2.35 (p = 0.013) | +3.65 (p = 0.002) | +1.30 |

In plain words: on ordinary days, when dealers (by any of the three guesses) held more positive gamma the evening before, the next day was calmer, which is what hedging theory predicts (dealers who are long gamma sell into rallies and buy into dips). On expiry days the sign flips: more positive gamma before expiry goes with a more volatile expiry day. One possible reading is that the gamma of the expiring contract is concentrated near the money and disappears during the day, so hedges are unwound in size. We have not tested that mechanism.

**Robustness to how GEX is scaled** (`results/real_day_level/r3_robustness.csv`). The ratio above has a problem: gross gamma peaks the evening before expiry (on average 1.1e12 rupees per 1% move with one trading day to expiry against 3.2e11 with five), and a 20-day median does not remove that weekly swing, so the regressor is mechanically about four times larger on expiry eves, which could feed the expiry interaction. Five variants remove that channel; all keep the regime controls. Holm-adjusted p-values are over all 30 GEX terms in the table.

| Variant | US convention: ordinary / extra on expiry (p) | Pro participant: ordinary / extra on expiry (p, Holm p) | Client mirror: ordinary / extra on expiry (p) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Per standard deviation | -0.040 (0.03) / +0.041 (0.11) | -0.053 (0.03) / +0.126 (0.002, Holm 0.04) | -0.071 (0.01) / +0.110 (0.002) |
| Sign only (GEX > 0) | +0.017 (0.73) / -0.024 (0.82) | -0.088 (0.04) / +0.332 (0.002, Holm 0.06) | -0.098 (0.50) / +0.131 (0.44) |
| Net / gross of the same chain | +0.27 (0.35) / +0.23 (0.62) | -3.16 (0.004) / +8.21 (0.001, Holm 0.04) | -0.50 (0.71) / +3.11 (0.09) |
| Same, plus gross gamma controls | +0.31 (0.27) / +0.23 (0.61) | -3.06 (0.004) / +8.14 (0.001, Holm 0.04) | -1.49 (0.25) / +3.86 (0.03) |
| Same, before the closing auction | +0.37 (0.23) / +0.32 (0.52) | -3.51 (0.001) / +8.94 (0.0005, Holm 0.02) | -0.69 (0.61) / +3.27 (0.08) |

So the sign flip is robust under the Pro rule: with a scale-free measure, with gross gamma controlled, and without the auction days, and its expiry term survives the Holm correction in four of five variants (the sign-only version gives Holm p = 0.06). Under the US rule it disappears once GEX is scale-free, and under the Client mirror it weakens to borderline. In the Pro sign-only version, an evening with positive Pro GEX goes with about 8% lower variance the next ordinary day and about 28% higher variance the next expiry day ($e^{-0.088} - 1$ and $e^{0.244} - 1$).

How much to trust this: (1) the sign of dealer positions is not observed, only guessed; the Pro and Client weights come from all index options, not Nifty strikes; (2) it is in-sample, and R1 shows the same information does not improve out-of-sample forecasts; (3) we tried 3 sign rules, with and without controls, in six versions of the GEX measure (the Holm column above accounts for the robustness table, not for every version we looked at); (4) positioning may respond to expected volatility (reverse causality). With the regime controls, adding GEX raises $R^2$ only from 0.467 to between 0.469 and 0.474.

**After the falsification tests** ([FALSIFICATION.md](FALSIFICATION.md), REAL DATA). The ordinary-day column does not survive: a 20-day block shuffle of the GEX series gives p = 0.25, GEX from later days predicts today's volatility at least as well as yesterday's, 91.9% of randomly drawn sign rules give a significant coefficient, and gross gamma alone gives t = -5.3. It is a slow-moving level effect (more total option gamma, calmer markets), not evidence about which way dealers hedge. The expiry-day flip under the Pro rule survives the shuffles, the random sign rules and the timing horse race, but it is carried by the daily Pro positioning weights, makes out-of-sample forecasts about 5% worse, and does not replicate on Bank Nifty (p = 0.25). Treat it as a lead to test on new data, not as a result.

**How much power did R3 have?** ([POWER_REAL.md](POWER_REAL.md), SIMULATIONS CALIBRATED ON REAL DATA.) With 937 days, the Pro-share effects these regressions detect 80% of the time are about 3.0 (ordinary days) and 6.9 to 7.1 (expiry interaction), just below the estimates (-3.16 and 8.21). Effects half as large would need about 3.1 to 3.9 times the sample (8.0 to 10.9 more years), and a GEX sign wrong on 25% of random days cuts power at the estimate to 23% and 27%.

On expiry days, does the official close move toward the strike with the largest open interest (or the max-pain strike, both from the previous evening's chain) more than on other days? File: `results/real_day_level/r4_pinning.csv`. No: the change in distance to the max-OI strike does not differ between expiry days and other days (Mann-Whitney p = 0.67), and for max pain p = 0.12. This is a crude check. The dedicated study (`docs/PINNING_STUDY.md`) measures distance to the nearest strike instead and finds pinning in some periods only: Nifty closed 4.5 points closer to a strike on expiry days from November 2024 to August 2025 (p = 0.0006), not at all since the move to Tuesday, and Bank Nifty pinned while it had weekly options (p = 0.008). None of the dealer-gamma proxies explains when pinning happens.

## R5. End-of-day momentum and dealer gamma

**The question.** In US markets the return earlier in the day tends to continue into the last 30 minutes ("intraday momentum": Gao, Han, Li and Zhou 2018 found it for the S&P 500 ETF using the first half hour; Baltussen, Da, Lammers and Martens 2021 used the rest-of-day return in futures markets, the design we copy), and Baltussen et al. argue that part of the reason is option dealers who are short gamma: they have to buy after a rise and sell after a fall, and much of that trading happens near the close. If that holds in India, the effect should be stronger when dealers were short gamma the evening before.

**Setup.** For each day, the "rest of day" return runs from the previous day's last 1-minute close to the 14:59 bar close (so it includes the overnight gap), and the "last 30 minutes" return runs from 14:59 to 15:29. Both are from the validated Nifty 1-minute bars and are in basis points. We regress the last-30-minute return on the rest-of-day return, its product with an indicator for "GEX was negative at the previous close", and that indicator. Newey-West standard errors with 5 lags. 932 days, 198 of them expiry days; the GEX regressions use the 929 of them (197 expiry) on which GEX at the previous close exists. Files: `results/momentum_gex/regressions.csv`, `gamma_states.csv`, `stability.csv` and `results/momentum_gex_stdout.txt`.

**Result 1: no momentum, a mild reversal.** On all days the slope is -0.029 (p = 0.015): a 100 bp rise over the day is followed, on average, by a 2.9 bp fall in the last 30 minutes. The slope is about the same on expiry days (-0.037, p = 0.019). So Nifty does not show the US pattern at all over 2023 to 2026.

**Result 2: the reversal is concentrated on negative-GEX days, under two of the three sign rules.** The table shows the extra slope on days after negative GEX (the hedging story predicts a positive number).

| Sign rule | Days with GEX < 0 | Extra slope when GEX < 0 (p) | Same, with regime-specific slopes (p) | Same, with VIX and move-size controls (p) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| US convention | 107 | +0.013 (0.58) | +0.002 (0.95) | -0.001 (0.97) |
| Pro participant | 463 | -0.068 (0.0002) | -0.070 (0.0007) | -0.050 (0.003) |

Source: `results/momentum_gex/regressions.csv` (929 days with a previous close, GEX and a full last 30 minutes; rerun from the raw files on 3 November 2026 with the same result). The falsification study's version on 932 days (`results/falsification/t7_family_A_original_tests.csv`) differs by at most 0.001 in each coefficient; an earlier version of this table had mixed the two.

In plain words: under the Pro rule, after a negative-GEX evening, a 100 bp move over the day is followed by about a 5.7 bp move the other way in the last 30 minutes; after a positive-GEX evening there is essentially no follow-through either way (slope +0.011, p = 0.29). The Pro result has the same sign in all three expiry regimes and in both halves of the sample (`stability.csv`; p between 0.0005 and 0.17 in the regimes, 0.035 and 0.011 in the halves). Across all 36 GEX interaction tests in `regressions.csv` (three rules, four specifications, three day subsets), the Pro result keeps a Holm-adjusted p of 0.007 (plain), 0.021 (regime slopes) and 0.077 (VIX and size controls); Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted 0.003, 0.005 and 0.010. The Client-mirror result disappears once VIX and the size of the day's move are controlled for: it rests on only 38 days, most of them volatile ones, so it is better read as "big, volatile days reverse more" than as a gamma effect.

**Why this is puzzling.** Hedging theory says dealers who are long gamma trade against the market (which produces reversal) and dealers who are short gamma trade with it (momentum). If the Pro-based sign were right, negative GEX should mean momentum, but we see the strongest reversal there. If instead the Pro sign were backwards, R5 would fit the theory, but then R3's ordinary-day result (higher Pro GEX, calmer next day) would go against it. Both cannot hold under one simple story. Three readings we have not yet separated: (a) the Pro category's net option position is not a good proxy for who hedges Nifty options (it pools all index options and includes proprietary traders who may not delta-hedge at all); (b) negative Pro GEX picks out market conditions (for example, heavy retail call buying) in which the close reverses for reasons that have nothing to do with hedging; (c) chance, since we tried three rules and several specifications. The falsification tests ([FALSIFICATION.md](FALSIFICATION.md)) rule out (c) as far as data can: the Pro result survives shuffled GEX, placebo dates, random sign rules and a pre-registered out-of-sample test. They also weaken R3, whose ordinary-day term turned out to be a proxy for gross gamma. Estimating the sign from the data ([DEALER_SIGN.md](DEALER_SIGN.md)) puts the end-of-day dealers at the reverse of the Pro sign, which is reading (a) or (b), not a confirmation of the hedging story.

**Closing auction.** From 3 August 2026 the 15:29 bar is the auction price after 13 frozen minutes, which changes what the last-30-minute return means. Without those 63 days the results are the same: overall slope -0.030 (p = 0.017), Pro interaction -0.072 (p = 0.0001) and -0.053 (p = 0.001) with controls (`results/closing_auction/r5_without_auction.csv`).

**Caveats.** Before costs, not a trading result: the average "follow the day's direction" payoff on negative-Pro-GEX days is -2.1 bp per day (t = -2.3), smaller than a typical round-trip cost in Nifty futures plus slippage. Before 3 August 2026 the 15:29 bar close is the last trade, not NSE's official close (then a 30-minute average); from that date it is the auction price and equals the official close.
