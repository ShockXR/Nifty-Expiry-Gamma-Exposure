# Pitch: what dealer positioning adds to your expiry-day result

*For Prof. Piyush Pandey. From Shreyan Thanki, November 2026. Every result below is from **real NSE data**, 2 January 2023 to 2 November 2026 (942 trading days, 200 Nifty expiries), unless it says otherwise. Each one links to a document in this repository that shows the file it comes from.*

## The idea in three sentences

Your 2025 paper with Chhimwal and V. Pandey shows that each index became more volatile and more heavily traded on its own expiry day after NSE separated the Nifty and Bank Nifty expiries. I tested one candidate explanation, option dealers' gamma hedging, with public NSE files, first by asking whether dealer positioning improves out-of-sample forecasts beyond your mcsGARCH model, and then by trying hard to break every in-sample link I found. The answer is more interesting than a yes or a no, and it gives a concrete plan for a paper.

## What I found

1. **Your "after" sample replicates almost exactly, and I think I found your "before" window.** With 360 minutes a day I get exactly your 21,239 Nifty returns and Table 1's extremes to the printed digit; mcsGARCH gives alpha 0.0498 and beta 0.9407 (printed 0.0494 and 0.9410). The stated "before" period (November 2023 to February 2024) has 82 trading days, not 64, and does not match. Scoring every 64-day window against Table 1 picks out **14 November 2022 to 10 February 2023**, which reproduces both printed "before" maxima to every decimal and agrees on statistics not used in the search. My first question to you is whether that was the sample. ([REPLICATION_NSE.md](docs/REPLICATION_NSE.md))
2. **Your model is a strong benchmark; at short horizons it can be improved, mostly by giving it more information.** In an out-of-sample contest on 792 test days (your paper fits mcsGARCH but does not forecast with it, so these forecasts come from my implementation, refitted as often as every other model), a hybrid that keeps mcsGARCH and corrects its errors with a gradient-boosted model, using the previous evening's option chain, India VIX and calendar features, forecasts the next 15 and 30 minutes 5.8% and 7.6% better (QLIKE, Diebold-Mariano p below 0.001), also before NSE's closing auction and on a later hold-out. It survives a correction for having tried 31 challengers (Hansen's SPA test) and, most importantly, an mcsGARCH given the same information (India VIX or option-implied variance as its daily level): 4.8% (p 0.005) and 5.9% (p below 0.001) better. In the last hour the 6.4% gain is mostly information, not method: mcsGARCH with VIX as its daily level comes within 1.7% (p 0.23), and on expiry days nothing beats your model in the last hour after that correction. Nor is the hybrid structure shown to matter, since plain gradient boosting does about as well, and under squared-error loss the models cannot be ranked. ([MODEL_ITERATIONS_REAL.md](docs/MODEL_ITERATIONS_REAL.md); [REFEREE_RESPONSE_FORECAST.md](docs/REFEREE_RESPONSE_FORECAST.md))
3. **The expiry effect depends on the expiry regime, and lives at the end of the day.** Compared with the rest of its own week, a Nifty expiry day carried about 11% more variance before November 2024 (weak evidence: a simulation check shows the test over-rejects in that period), no clear excess under the single Thursday weekly (November 2024 to August 2025), and about 21% more since the move to Tuesday, concentrated in the last hour (about 43% more; on a stricter set of clean weeks the whole-day part is near zero, the last-hour part is not). When Bank Nifty's expiry moved to Wednesday, its last-30-minute premium moved with it (+1.14 log points, placebo p 0.005) while whole-day variance did not; and a synthetic-control study of the November 2024 cut in weekly options finds that NSE sector indices with no options at all were also more volatile on Wednesdays, so whole-day weekday premia are not clean evidence of an expiry effect (that study is too imprecise to say more, [SYNTHETIC_CONTROL.md](docs/SYNTHETIC_CONTROL.md)). ([REAL_DATA_RESULTS.md](docs/REAL_DATA_RESULTS.md), R2; [EVENT_STUDY.md](docs/EVENT_STUDY.md))
4. **Dealer gamma, measured from public files, does not help forecasts; one in-sample pattern held up under most tests, but it is a Pro-positioning pattern rather than a gamma one.** GEX features add nothing out of sample under any of three sign rules. In sample, Nifty reverses into the close (a 100 bp move over the day is followed by about 3 bp the other way in the last 30 minutes). That alone counts against the simplest hedging story: if dealers were short all options, their hedging would make the close continue the day's move, and it does not, nor does the reversal grow with the amount of gamma. The reversal appears only after evenings when GEX built from NSE's "Pro" participant positions was negative (slope -0.057 against +0.011 after positive evenings; difference p = 0.0002). It survived block shuffles of GEX, GEX taken from later days, random sign rules (it beats 97% of them, 99.6% with controls) and a split-sample test on 2025 to 2026 data whose direction came from 2023 to 2024 (p = 0.006, though the split was chosen after the full-sample result was known). It survives a Holm correction over the 78 headline tests (p = 0.016) but not over the falsification study's full family of 742 tests. The negative-Pro evenings come in long runs (95% of 2023 evenings, about one in five from 2025), so the honest sample is a few dozen episodes rather than 466 days; with inference built for that, the pattern holds in the full sample (largest p 0.012) and from 2025 (0.038) but not inside 2023 to 2024. Two caveats change its reading: it is carried by the day-to-day changes in Pro participants' net option positions, not by the shape of the option chain, so it is better described as a Pro-positioning pattern than a dealer-gamma result; and as a trade it earns about 4 bp before costs and is indistinguishable from zero after them (it breaks even at about 0.5 bp of slippage per side, so the verdict depends on that assumption). ([FALSIFICATION.md](docs/FALSIFICATION.md), [F2_ECONOMICS.md](docs/F2_ECONOMICS.md), [REFEREE_RESPONSE_F2.md](docs/REFEREE_RESPONSE_F2.md))
5. **The sign of dealer positions is the real open problem.** The robust reversal fits hedging theory only if the Pro positions are the opposite of dealers' positions, while the next-day volatility link fits only if they are the same. When I estimate the sign from the market's response instead of assuming it, the end-of-day outcome points clearly to the reverse of the Pro positions, while the volatility outcome is too weak to point anywhere (so the two cannot be shown to disagree), and no estimated sign beats the fixed rules out of sample. The ordinary-day volatility link also turned out to be a proxy for the total amount of option gamma, not for its sign. ([DEALER_SIGN.md](docs/DEALER_SIGN.md))
6. **Pinning is real but regime-specific.** Under the single Thursday weekly, Nifty's official close on expiry day was 4.5 points closer to a 50-point strike than on other days (p = 0.0006); since the move to Tuesday there is none, and pinning is unrelated to any measure of dealer gamma. A test of six market-structure explanations, with its analysis plan committed before its results were computed (on the same data that showed the pattern), (open interest near the money, the expiring contract's share of trading, contract size, the number of weeklies, open interest held into expiry rather than rolled, strike spacing) found that none explains when pinning comes and goes; only open interest held into expiry matters, and only day to day. ([PINNING_STUDY.md](docs/PINNING_STUDY.md), [PINNING_MECHANISM.md](docs/PINNING_MECHANISM.md))

I also froze a forecast for Tuesday 3 November's expiry in the repository before the market opened, so it can be scored against what actually happened ([results/live/2026-11-03/forecast.md](results/live/2026-11-03/forecast.md)).

## Points from your paper I would like to check with you

Offered as a careful reader's questions, each now checked on real data:

1. **The "before" window** (point 1 above).
2. **Connectedness scale.** Tables 3 and 4 report the sum of the two cross shares, which is twice the standard Diebold-Yilmaz index for two variables (84.00 corresponds to 42.00). Comparisons within the paper are unaffected. The overall fall in connectedness replicates (87.9 to 74.9 and 68.9 on your scale; printed 84.00 to 76.38 and 73.02), but the first- and last-hour totals of 99.42 and 105.22 are outside everything a 40-setting grid of unreported VAR choices produces.
3. **Minute-level tests.** The Mann-Whitney tests in Tables 5 and 6 treat thousands of minutes from a few dozen days as independent. On real data the minute-level test gives p-values as small as 10^-256; tests on whole days keep the Nifty findings (higher volatility overall and on Thursdays) but not the Bank Nifty Wednesday result (+16%, not significant at the day level).
4. **Persistence.** Your Table 2 implies the half-life of an intraday volatility shock fell from about 339 to 72 minutes for Nifty. I find 359 to 72 on the inferred window, with a wide interval on the "before" side (197 to 1,255 minutes), so the fall is clear but its size is uncertain.
5. **A few printed numbers look like typesetting slips** (for example, Table 4's first-hour Nifty rows sum to 101, and one Anderson-Darling statistic in Table 1 is printed with a comma for the decimal point). A separate AI agent re-typed all 223 printed numbers blind from the PDF; 222 matched our first typing and the one difference is that comma, so these points are in the printed paper, not our transcription ([REKEY_CHECK.md](docs/REKEY_CHECK.md)).

## What I would ask for

* **Your view on the "Pro" category** in NSE's participant-wise open interest as a proxy for dealers. My results suggest it is not a clean one, and this is where your knowledge of the Indian market would matter most.
* **Data:** the exact "before" sample dates, and whether your group has intraday option-chain snapshots (positions during the day, not only at the close), which would let me test the hedging story directly on expiry afternoons.
